169 Wentworth Street Sold for $7.4 Million: What This Sale Tells Us About Charleston's Luxury Market

One of downtown Charleston's most storied private residences changed hands. 169 Wentworth Street sold for $7.4 million. Jimmy Dye, co-founder of The Cassina Group, represented the buyer of the historic Harleston Village property.  


What Makes 169 Wentworth Street So Significant?

Known historically as the Corbett-Irving House, this Greek Revival residence in Harleston Village dates to the mid-1830s. It sits tucked behind a gated entrance beneath mature live oaks, the kind of setting that makes you forget you're in the middle of a city. "The moment you step through the gate of 169 Wentworth, you understand how rare this property is," said Jimmy Dye, “I am thrilled to have helped my buyer secure such an iconic residence." 


Charleston architect Eddie Fava led the architectural reimagining, and Lauren Sanchez Design Ltd. handled the interiors. The result is a home with soaring ceilings, a dramatic steel-framed glass wall overlooking the gardens, a grand foyer, formal dining room, expansive living room, private study, and a chef's kitchen with a large island, fireplace, bar, and butler's pantry. 


Outside, landscape architect Glen Gardner designed a series of intimate garden rooms connected by brick pathways. There's a pool and a fully equipped pool house with a private living area, bedroom, bathroom, and covered porch. In downtown Charleston, that kind of private outdoor space is genuinely rare.


Harleston Village has long been one of Charleston's most coveted neighborhoods for exactly this reason: it offers the walkability and architectural richness of the city's historic core. Properties like 169 Wentworth Street is where historic integrity meets modern livability at that scale.


What This Sale Says About Demand for Historic Charleston Properties

This transaction reflects something Dye has been watching closely across the Charleston luxury market: demand for exceptional historic properties in downtown's most coveted neighborhoods remains strong. Buyers at this level aren't just purchasing square footage. They're purchasing craftsmanship, location, and a connection to Charleston's history that simply is unmatched. 


Harleston Village, South of Broad, and the French Quarter all share that quality. These are neighborhoods where the character is protected, and where the pool of truly exceptional properties is small enough that when one becomes available, serious buyers move decisively.


Jimmy Dye works across all of these markets, and others, from the barrier islands of Sullivan's Island and Isle of Palms to Old Village in Mount Pleasant, to the historic streets of downtown Charleston.
